Criminal Injuries Compensation (CICA) Claims Explained

7 min read Updated August 2026 England, Scotland & Wales

Being the victim of a violent crime is traumatic enough without also having to navigate a compensation process — but many people don't realise the state operates a dedicated scheme for exactly this situation, entirely separate from suing an attacker personally or the outcome of any criminal case.

What is the CICA scheme?

The Criminal Injuries Compensation Authority (CICA) administers a government-funded scheme that provides compensation to innocent victims of violent crime in England, Scotland and Wales (a separate scheme applies in Northern Ireland). It covers injuries — physical or psychological — resulting from crimes such as assault, sexual offences, and other violent crimes, and it's entirely funded by the state rather than requiring anyone to sue the offender personally, which matters a great deal since offenders are often unidentified or have no money to pay compensation even if sued.

Who can claim

Direct victims of a crime of violence can generally apply, and in some circumstances so can close relatives of someone who died as a result of a crime of violence, or a person who witnessed and was psychologically affected by a violent crime against a loved one. Certain factors can reduce or bar an award — for example, having a relevant unspent criminal conviction, or not having cooperated reasonably with the police or the CICA's own investigation.

Report to the police as soon as you reasonably can

CICA generally expects incidents to have been reported to the police without unreasonable delay, and for the applicant to cooperate with any police investigation. This doesn't mean an offender needs to be caught or convicted for a claim to succeed — but a police report is usually essential evidence that a crime of violence occurred.

Time limits

Applications generally need to be made within two years of the date of the incident. Extensions are possible in limited circumstances — for example, where the applicant was under 18 at the time of the incident (the clock effectively runs differently for children), or where there are exceptional reasons the application couldn't reasonably have been made sooner. Given how strict this deadline can be in practice, it's important not to delay in seeking advice.

How compensation is calculated

Unlike a personal injury claim against a negligent defendant, CICA compensation is calculated using a fixed tariff scheme — a published list of injury types with set compensation bands, rather than an individually negotiated settlement. Awards can also include compensation for loss of earnings in more serious cases (subject to a minimum period out of work), and for financial dependency or bereavement where a victim has died. Awards are subject to an overall maximum and minimum threshold, meaning very minor injuries may not qualify at all.

Making a claim

Applications are made directly to CICA using their standard application form, setting out details of the incident, the police reference (crime number), and supporting medical evidence of the injury. CICA can request further evidence and, where necessary, medical reports. If an application is refused or the award is disputed, there's a right to ask CICA to review the decision, and — if still unsuccessful — to appeal to the First-tier Tribunal.

This guide describes the general position on the CICA scheme as at the date above and is for general information only — it is not legal advice. Time limits are strict and the process has many procedural pitfalls, so get in touch as soon as possible if you've been the victim of a violent crime.
