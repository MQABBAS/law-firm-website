Prenuptial Agreements in the UK: Are They Legally Binding?

7 min read Updated August 2026 England & Wales

Prenuptial agreements have a reputation problem in the UK — many people still believe they're unromantic, or simply unenforceable. Neither is quite true. While not automatically binding in the way they are in some countries, a well-drafted prenup carries real legal weight in England and Wales.

Their legal status

Unlike a straightforward contract, a prenuptial agreement isn't automatically enforceable in England and Wales — the court always retains ultimate discretion over financial matters on divorce, and no private agreement can completely remove that. However, this doesn't mean prenups are worthless. Courts now give them substantial weight, provided certain conditions are met.

The Radmacher case

The turning point was the 2010 Supreme Court decision in Radmacher v Granatino, which held that courts should give effect to a nuptial agreement that was freely entered into by each party, with a full appreciation of its implications, unless it would not be fair to hold the parties to it in the circumstances prevailing at the time of the divorce. In practice, this means a properly prepared prenup is now very likely to be followed, especially where both parties are financially sophisticated and had good advice.

What makes one more likely to be upheld

Courts look favourably on prenups where:

  • Both parties received independent legal advice before signing
  • There was full and honest financial disclosure by both sides beforehand
  • The agreement was signed with reasonable time before the wedding — signing days before can suggest pressure
  • The terms don't leave either party, or any children, in a position of genuine need
  • Neither party was under undue pressure or duress to sign
Timing matters more than people expect

A prenup signed the week before the wedding is far more vulnerable to challenge than one signed months in advance — not because of a strict legal rule, but because last-minute signing raises questions about whether someone genuinely felt free not to sign.

What a prenup can't do

A prenup cannot oust the court's jurisdiction over child maintenance — this remains protected regardless of what the agreement says. It also can't guarantee an outcome that leaves a party or children without their reasonable needs met; a court will depart from an otherwise valid agreement if strictly enforcing it would produce genuine unfairness or hardship at the time of the divorce.

Who typically gets one

Prenups are increasingly common well beyond the ultra-wealthy — they're often used to protect pre-marital assets such as a house bought before the relationship, an inheritance, a family business, or to record how finances will be kept separate or combined during the marriage. A postnuptial agreement (signed after marriage) achieves much the same thing for couples who didn't arrange one beforehand.

This guide describes the general legal position on prenuptial agreements in England & Wales as at the date above and is for general information only — it is not legal advice. Whether an agreement will be upheld depends heavily on how it's drafted and the circumstances of signing, so professional advice is essential.
