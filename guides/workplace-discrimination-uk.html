Workplace Discrimination: Your Rights Under the Equality Act 2010

8 min read Updated August 2026 Great Britain

The Equality Act 2010 brought together a patchwork of earlier discrimination laws into a single framework covering the workplace, and everyday life more broadly. Understanding how it actually works — the different types of discrimination it covers, and how claims are brought — helps you recognise when something has genuinely crossed a legal line.

Protected characteristics

The Equality Act protects against discrimination related to nine protected characteristics: age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, and sexual orientation. Crucially, protection isn't limited to existing employees — it extends to job applicants during recruitment, and in some respects to former employees too.

Types of discrimination

The Act recognises several distinct forms:

  • Direct discrimination — treating someone less favourably specifically because of a protected characteristic.
  • Indirect discrimination — applying a policy or practice that looks neutral on its face, but which puts people sharing a protected characteristic at a particular disadvantage, without objective justification.
  • Discrimination arising from disability — treating a disabled person unfavourably because of something connected to their disability, rather than the disability itself.
  • Failure to make reasonable adjustments — not taking reasonable steps to remove a disadvantage a disabled employee faces because of their disability.

Harassment and victimisation

Harassment covers unwanted conduct related to a protected characteristic that violates someone's dignity, or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment. Victimisation covers being treated badly because you've made or supported a discrimination complaint, or otherwise done something protected under the Act — importantly, this protects people who raise genuine concerns, even if the underlying discrimination complaint doesn't ultimately succeed.

No minimum service required

Unlike ordinary unfair dismissal, discrimination protection doesn't require any minimum length of service — it applies from day one, and can even apply before employment technically starts, during recruitment.

Bringing a claim

As with most employment tribunal claims, you generally need to notify ACAS for early conciliation before lodging a claim, within the same 3-months-less-a-day time limit that applies to unfair dismissal — see our guide on unfair dismissal claims for more on that process, which runs in parallel here. It's often worth raising a grievance internally first, both to try to resolve things directly and because how an employer responds can itself become relevant evidence.

Compensation

Unlike ordinary unfair dismissal compensation, discrimination compensation is uncapped. It typically includes compensation for financial losses (such as lost earnings) and an award for injury to feelings, assessed using established banding guidelines (often referred to as the "Vento bands") that set a rough range depending on the seriousness of the discrimination.

This guide describes the general framework of the Equality Act 2010 as at the date above and is for general information only — it is not legal advice. Discrimination claims are often fact-sensitive and time-limited, so get advice promptly if you think something may have happened.
