Postnuptial Agreements in the UK Explained

6 min read Updated August 2026 England & Wales

Not every couple thinks about a formal financial agreement before the wedding — and life doesn't stop changing once you're married. A postnuptial agreement offers a way to put clear, considered terms in place at any point during a marriage, not just before it.

What is a postnuptial agreement?

A postnuptial agreement is a written agreement entered into after a couple has already married or formed a civil partnership, recording how they intend their finances, property, and other assets to be dealt with if they later separate or divorce. It serves broadly the same purpose as a prenuptial agreement, but is signed after the wedding rather than before it — sometimes years into a marriage.

Why couples use one

Postnuptial agreements are used for a range of reasons: a significant change in financial circumstances during the marriage (an inheritance, a business becoming successful, a large gift from family), the couple simply never having got around to a prenup before marrying, resolving underlying tension about finances in a structured way, or protecting a specific asset — such as a family business or a property owned before the marriage — that has become more relevant over time.

Sometimes used to help a struggling marriage, not just plan for its end

Some couples enter a postnuptial agreement as part of working through a difficult period in their marriage — providing reassurance and clarity that can, ironically, help a relationship continue, rather than being purely a preparation for divorce.

How binding is it?

Like prenuptial agreements, postnuptial agreements are not automatically binding on a court in the way an ordinary commercial contract would be — English family law preserves the court's ultimate discretion over financial matters on divorce. However, following the landmark Supreme Court decision in Radmacher v Granatino, courts will generally give a nuptial agreement (whether pre- or post-marriage) decisive weight and hold the parties to it, provided it was freely entered into with a full understanding of its implications, and it wouldn't be unfair to do so in the circumstances at the time of divorce.

Getting it right

Certain factors dramatically increase the chances a postnuptial agreement will be upheld: both parties receiving independent legal advice before signing, full and honest financial disclosure from both sides, no undue pressure to sign (including a lack of time pressure), and terms that remain broadly fair at the point of divorce — particularly ensuring any children's needs are properly met regardless of what the agreement says about the adults' finances.

Postnup vs prenup

The legal principles courts apply are essentially the same for both, but timing matters practically: a prenuptial agreement is signed under the (sometimes unhelpful) pressure of an approaching wedding date, while a postnuptial agreement can be negotiated calmly, without that particular time pressure — something courts may view favourably when assessing whether it was freely entered into.
