Resolving Commercial Contract Disputes Under UK Law

8 min read Updated August 2026 England & Wales

A supplier misses deadlines, a client refuses to pay, terms are interpreted differently by each side — commercial contract disputes are one of the most common legal issues businesses face. Here's how they're typically resolved, and what your options are at each stage.

  2. First steps when a dispute arises
  3. Alternative dispute resolution
  4. Court proceedings

What counts as a breach

A breach of contract happens when a party fails to perform an obligation without lawful excuse — whether that's not delivering at all, delivering late, or delivering something that doesn't meet the agreed standard. English law distinguishes between a breach of a condition (a fundamental term, breach of which can allow the innocent party to end the contract and claim damages) and a breach of warranty (a lesser term, which typically only gives rise to a damages claim).

First steps when a dispute arises

Before anything formal, it's worth: reviewing the contract itself carefully (including any dispute resolution or notice clauses), gathering correspondence and evidence of the breach and its impact, and sending a clear written notice setting out the issue and what you want to happen. Many disputes resolve at this stage without needing to escalate further.

Alternative dispute resolution

If direct discussion doesn't resolve things, alternative dispute resolution (ADR) is usually the next step, and English courts actively expect parties to consider it before litigating:

  • Negotiation — structured discussions, often through solicitors.
  • Mediation — a neutral third party helps both sides reach a voluntary settlement.
  • Arbitration — a private, binding process, common where the contract includes an arbitration clause.
Refusing ADR can cost you — even if you win

Courts can penalise a party in costs for unreasonably refusing to engage with ADR, even if that party ultimately wins the case at trial. It's rarely a neutral decision to ignore a genuine offer to mediate.

Court proceedings

If ADR fails or isn't appropriate, a claim can be issued in the County Court or High Court depending on value and complexity. Lower-value claims may fall within the small claims track — see our dedicated guide on small claims court. Larger commercial disputes typically proceed through detailed pleadings, disclosure of documents, witness evidence, and ultimately trial if not settled beforehand (the vast majority of cases settle before reaching that point).

Remedies available

The most common remedy is damages — financial compensation intended to put the innocent party in the position they'd have been in had the contract been performed properly. Other remedies exist for specific situations, including specific performance (a court order requiring the breaching party to actually perform the contract) and injunctions (orders preventing a party from doing something), though these are less commonly granted than damages.

This guide describes general principles of English contract law as at the date above and is for general information only — it is not legal advice. Contract disputes are highly fact-specific, so please get in touch to discuss the details of your situation.
